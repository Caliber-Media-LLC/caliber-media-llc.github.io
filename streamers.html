Why streamers use voice

7 REASONS IT BELONGS ON YOUR STREAM

  1. A character that answers back

    Pick a voice pack and your commands get a reply in character. It's a running bit your chat can enjoy — your game talks back to you.

  2. Viewers hear it with no extra setup

    Replies play through your PC's sound, so your stream picks them up with your desktop audio, like any other game sound.

  3. Talk to chat without firing commands

    Use push-to-talk and CaliberEngine only listens while you hold one key. Talk to chat freely the rest of the time.

  4. Pause it on air

    Say mute and it stops acting on what you say — for a story, an ad read, or a guest. Say unmute when you're back in the game.

  5. Hands stay on the game

    Say reload or quickslot one and the key is pressed for you. Less fumbling for keys while you're reading chat.

  6. Health callouts out loud

    Show it where your health bar is once, and it calls out when you drop past 75, 50 and 25 percent. Great tension for viewers, and you don't have to look away.

  7. Pick a rating that fits your channel

    Every voice pack carries a rating — G, PG, PG-13 or R — so you know what you're bringing on air before you go live.

Straight talk

BEFORE YOU GO LIVE

  • Always-listening mode hears everything you say, including talk with chat. On stream, push-to-talk is the safer choice.
  • Speech recognition is English only, on Windows 10 and 11. A noisy room or a poor microphone can make it mishear you.
  • Some competitive games with strict anti-cheat may not allow programs that send keys. Check a game's rules before ranked play.
  • The voices are AI-generated characters made with ElevenLabs from Caliber Media's scripts — not recordings of real people.
  • It's a beta. If something misbehaves on stream, tell us in the Discord.
